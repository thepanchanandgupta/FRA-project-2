Section 1 · Introduction and objectives

What we studied, and how

What is credit risk?

The risk that borrowers fail to repay. Lenders report it through GNPA (share of loans gone bad), NNPA (after provisions), PCR (how much of the bad loans is covered by provisions) and CRAR (capital cushion against losses).

Objectives

Track asset quality, provisioning and capital for each lender; compare each with its closest peer; and separate lender-specific trends from shocks that hit everyone, such as COVID-19.

Method in brief

Annual data for FY2017–FY2026 extracted from annual reports. Banks are compared with banks and NBFCs with NBFCs. Missing data stays blank; nothing is estimated.

Banks vs NBFCs: what differs

  • Scale measure: banks report advances; NBFCs report AUM (assets under management). They are not the same thing.
  • Funding and capital: banks use deposits and Basel III (CRAR, CET1); NBFCs borrow from markets and banks and report CRAR under NBFC rules.
  • Provision coverage: HDFC's PCR excludes written-off assets; Chola's PCR in its investor deck is Stage-3 based; definitions differ.
  • NPA vs Stage 3: legacy RBI NPA and Ind AS Stage 3 are related but not interchangeable.
4a · Systematic vs idiosyncratic risk

Nine risk events

Systematic = affects all lenders; idiosyncratic = specific to one lender. Open an event for the justification, mechanism, affected lenders and annual credit indicators before and after. These describe timing; they do not prove cause.

4c · Correlation analysis

Do share prices move with credit-risk indicators?

Method: Pearson and Spearman correlations of each stock's financial-year price return (Adj Close, last session on or before 31 March) with the change in a credit ratio over the same year (and the next year). Changes, not levels, are used so that trends do not create false correlation. Samples are 5 to 9 years, so only very strong relationships can be detected; shaded rows have p < 0.05 and are not corrected for the many tests run. Correlation is not causation.
